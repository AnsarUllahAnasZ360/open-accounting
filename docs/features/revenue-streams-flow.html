Revenue Streams

See which part of your business actually makes money. Tag the money you receive and spend to a stream — a part of your business like Web Dev, Digital Marketing, or AI Dev — and OpenBooks shows you the profit each one makes.

Before vs Now

Before

Streams were tied to your bookkeeping categories.

  • You labelled a whole category as a stream — everything in it counted as that one stream.
  • You couldn’t split a single payment between two services.
  • It only showed money coming in — not the profit.
  • You had to tag everything by hand, every time.
  • Invoices and bills weren’t included.
Now

You tag the actual payment or bill.

  • Open a payment and pick its stream — or split it across several.
  • OpenBooks remembers and starts tagging for you.
  • It shows real profit per stream (money in minus that work’s costs).
  • Shared costs like rent stay separate, so no stream looks unfairly bad.
  • Payments, invoices, and bills are all covered.

How you use it

  • 1Open a payment
    Click any transaction. You’ll see a “Revenue stream” box under its category.
  • ↓
  • 2Pick a stream — or split it
    Choose one stream for the whole amount, or split it (e.g. $500 Web Dev + $700 Marketing). A live counter makes sure the split adds up.
  • ↓
  • 3OpenBooks learns
    After you confirm the same client a few times, it starts tagging their future payments automatically.
  • ↓
  • 4See the results
    Open Revenue Streams in the sidebar for the profit of each stream — or just ask the built-in AI “Which stream made the most money this quarter?”

It gets smarter as you go

The first time, you tag a client’s payment yourself. The next time, OpenBooks suggests the same stream. After a few consistent confirmations, it just does it for you. If you ever correct it, it goes back to asking first — so it only automates what it’s sure about. Anything it’s unsure of waits in a “Needs Review” list you can approve with one click.
